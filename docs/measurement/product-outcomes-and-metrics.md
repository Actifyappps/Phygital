# Product outcomes, pilot metrics and value measurement

**Sources:** Concept Paper v1.1; Deliverables 003, 005, 012, 019, 020; 024 product blueprint; 024-R F-12.  
**Status:** Measurement framework only; baselines, owners and thresholds require Phase 0 evidence.

## 1. Separate activity from outcomes
Activity measures (registrations, listings, enquiries, uploads) do not prove value. Outcome measures must show whether buyers can assess supply and whether producers gain a fair, usable route to market.

## 2. Measurement groups
- Producer access and burden: time to onboard, out-of-pocket cost, repeat data entry, assisted-service dependency, connectivity failures.
- Supply and evidence quality: batches with complete claim-specific evidence, correction/rejection rates, evidence expiry and source traceability.
- Buyer usefulness: time to assess an offer, specification match, enquiry-to-qualified-response, buyer confidence and repeat intent.
- Commercial outcomes: accepted quotations, orders, completed transactions, gross-to-net producer proceeds, deductions, payment timing and disputes.
- Operational performance: cost per batch/order, hub service cost, support burden, exception volume/age, reconciliation time and on-time milestones.
- Integrity and safety: duplicate/withdrawn listing incidents, over-allocation attempts, recall trace coverage, abuse reports and appeal outcomes.
- Sustainability: recurring cost coverage, contribution by service, subsidy requirement and provider concentration.

## 3. Measurement definition
Each metric needs ID, purpose, numerator/denominator, inclusion/exclusion rules, source event/data, cadence, owner role, quality limitations, privacy classification and review. Distinguish leading indicators from lagging outcomes.

## 4. Targets
Set numeric targets only after baseline discovery and pilot route selection. Do not invent a conversion rate, transaction target or producer income improvement. Report sample size and uncertainty; suppress misleading comparisons when data is sparse.

## 5. Governance
Metrics must not become a proxy for staff performance where data quality or external factors dominate. Avoid public ranking of producers or hubs without consent, context and approved methodology.
